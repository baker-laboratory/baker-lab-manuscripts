# Explicit IPD and Baker Lab manuscript requirements

These are normative requirements derived from user-authorized internal Baker/IPD manuscript guidance. Apply them when relevant, but allow mandatory target-journal formatting to control submission mechanics. Source titles, URLs, exports, and access paths are intentionally withheld from this public release.

## Priority and terminology

- Use both physical IMRaD structure and conceptual opening-challenge-action-resolution logic when useful.
- Ensure every factual literature statement is cited and all references are collated.
- Match the target journal's article type, section order, length, figure, and formatting limits.
- Consult the IPD Style Guide for acronyms and abbreviations when it is available. Do not guess unavailable lab-specific acronym rules.

## Abstract or summary

Use the following rhetorical sequence unless the target journal requires another form:

1. Broad field context understandable across scientific disciplines.
2. More specific background for adjacent fields.
3. The general problem addressed by the study.
4. A direct statement of the main result.
5. Two or three sentences containing the key experiments and findings.
6. General context for the results.
7. Broader significance understandable outside the immediate specialty.

Treat this as a function-based scaffold, not a mandatory sentence count. Avoid claims that exceed the evidence.

## Baker Lab Introduction

Prepare the Introduction in this form before sending it to David for review:

- Paragraph 1: give only the relevant background, narrow to the unsolved problem, and end by stating that problem clearly.
- Paragraph 2: explain the rationale for solving the problem through protein design. A transition such as `We reasoned that...` is characteristic when it matches the logic.
- Optional paragraph 3: explain the essential design approach at a conceptual level.
- Do not summarize the results in these opening paragraphs. Results already belong in the abstract and Results section.

Use an inverted-pyramid progression: broad field, specific gap, study rationale. Do not turn the Introduction into a chronological literature review.

## Results

- Present results in the order that best supports the scientific story, not necessarily the order in which experiments were performed.
- Build the section around figures and tables. Introduce the data, then explain the robust inference.
- Use explanatory result headers that state the point of each section rather than generic procedural labels.
- Introduce every dataset that is later discussed.
- Keep claim strength commensurate with the data.
- Make observation, inference, and interpretation distinguishable. Do not lead the reader with an interpretation before presenting the data.
- Reserve broader speculation for the Discussion when the journal separates Results and Discussion.

## Discussion

Aim for a concise Discussion, commonly two or three paragraphs for a Baker-led methods/design paper, unless the journal or study requires more.

- Paragraph 1: interpret the key findings in the context of the field and include the strongest supporting evidence. Do not replay the Results section.
- Paragraph 2: compare with prior work; address major strengths, limitations, and unexpected findings.
- Paragraph 3: state the significance, remaining questions, and the most defensible future directions.

Use additional middle paragraphs only for distinct major points. Keep the Discussion shorter than the rest of the main scientific narrative. End with significance and forward direction rather than a second abstract.

## David-specific wording and methods notes

- Treat the final structure prediction for a designed sequence as the design model until an experimental structure is obtained; state this convention explicitly in Methods when applicable.
- When comparing an experimental structure with the computational model, write that the **design** agrees with or matches the experimental structure. Do not frame the news as a prediction matching a structure.
- Do not say genes were `ordered`. Prefer language such as `Synthetic genes encoding the designs were obtained from [vendor]` when factually correct.
- Put the gene vendor in Methods rather than the main narrative.
- Do not insert these phrases mechanically when the study does not involve designed sequences, predicted design models, synthetic genes, or experimental structures.

## Figures and captions

- Check the target journal's final dimensions before formatting.
- When journal rules permit, use Arial 9 pt for figure captions with a bold figure title and approximately Arial 6 pt for internal figure text.
- Use a consistent color system with stable semantic meaning. Suggested IPD colors are purple `#4b2e83`, cyan `#4196b5`, and magenta `#b41f85`; accessibility and journal requirements take priority.
- Make captions sufficiently self-contained to explain panels, encodings, samples, statistics, and abbreviations.
- Label axes and use appropriate major and minor ticks.
- Use structure orientation and zoom deliberately. Avoid unmodified default molecular-visualization colors and views.
- Reduce uninformative whitespace and simplify every figure around the scientific point it must communicate.
- Inspect figures at final print size.

## Acknowledgements and funding

- Confirm applicable funding for each IPD author through authorized internal records when available.
- Include only funding relevant to the work; do not create a laundry list.
- Record the sponsor award number or external reference, never an internal UW identifier beginning with `GF`, `GR`, `PG`, or `AWD`.
- Attribute each award to its recipient. Attach David to every award that is his.
- Always acknowledge the Howard Hughes Medical Institute when David Baker is an author.
- Ask every author to confirm their funding before submission.
- Do not infer funding from a previous paper. Recent papers may be used to identify candidates, but current authors must confirm applicability.

## Publication and administrative requirements

- All David Baker Lab publications require open access under a CC BY license.
- Papers with NIH funding must be deposited in PubMed Central.
- The primary author is responsible for confirming open-access and PMCID requirements.
- Keep the authorized internal manuscript record current and minimize piecemeal updates that generate repeated notifications.
- Confirm authorship using the applicable internal authorship policies.
- Consider professional line editing in advance of submission and notify the IPD communications contact at submission when appropriate.
